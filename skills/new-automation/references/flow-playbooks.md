# Flow playbooks

Starting points for the five flows most small businesses need first. Delays and message counts are defaults to adjust with the business's own data. They are not benchmarks.

General rules for every flow:

- One goal per flow and one call to action per message.
- Every flow has an exit condition: stop when the goal is reached or the person unsubscribes.
- Send an SMS only to people with explicit SMS consent. Use SMS for short, time-sensitive, high-value messages, and do not duplicate the email.
- Deliver what you promised in the signup form before you ask for anything.

## 1. Welcome series

- **Trigger:** a new subscriber or signup.
- **Goal:** a first purchase or first meaningful action.
- **Steps (starting point):**
  1. Immediately: welcome, deliver the promised thing (guide, discount, access), set expectations for what and how often they will get.
  2. Day 2 to 3: your story or best content, with real proof if you have it.
  3. Day 5 to 7: a soft call to action for the first action.
- **Exit:** the person converts or unsubscribes.
- **Primary metric:** first conversion.
- **Common mistakes:** not delivering the promised item in message 1, a hard sell in message 1, too many messages too fast.

## 2. Abandoned cart (online shops)

- **Trigger:** a cart was created and no purchase followed after a set time. This needs cart events from the shop platform.
- **Goal:** recover the purchase.
- **Steps (starting point):**
  1. About an hour later: a reminder with the items and a link straight back to the cart.
  2. About a day later: answer common objections such as shipping, returns and support.
  3. Two to three days later: an incentive only if the margin allows it.
- **Exit:** a purchase.
- **Primary metric:** recovered purchases or revenue.
- **Common mistakes:** offering a discount in the first message, which can teach customers to abandon on purpose. Not stopping the flow after a purchase.

## 3. Post-purchase and review request

- **Trigger:** an order was delivered (use the delivery date, not the order date).
- **Goal:** a happy customer, a review, and a repeat purchase.
- **Steps (starting point):**
  1. After delivery: thank you, what happens next, how to get the most from the product.
  2. After the customer has had time to use it: ask for a review with a direct link.
  3. Later, optional: a complementary product or a refill reminder.
- **Exit:** a review was left or a new order was placed.
- **Primary metric:** review rate and repeat purchase.
- **Common mistakes:** asking for a review before the product arrived. Rewarding only positive reviews, which can break platform rules and consumer law, so check the rules of the review platform and your country.

## 4. Win-back (re-engagement)

- **Trigger:** no purchase or activity for N days. Choose N from the normal buying cycle of the business.
- **Goal:** bring back inactive customers or clean the list.
- **Steps (starting point):**
  1. A useful check-in: what is new, no pressure.
  2. A reminder of the value, with an incentive only if the margin allows it.
  3. A last message: "Do you still want to hear from us?" with an easy way to stay or leave.
- **Exit:** the person is active again, or the flow ends and inactive contacts are removed or suppressed.
- **Primary metric:** reactivation (a click or purchase) and a cleaner list.
- **Common mistakes:** mailing inactive contacts forever, which can hurt deliverability. Using an old consent without checking that it still covers the message.

## 5. Lead nurture and trial onboarding (services, B2B, SaaS)

- **Trigger:** a lead magnet download or a trial signup.
- **Goal:** a booked call, a demo or an activated trial.
- **Steps (starting point):**
  1. Deliver the resource or access and state the single next step.
  2. Teach something useful about the problem the lead has.
  3. Show a real example or proof. Use only real customers and results.
  4. Invite them to a call, a demo or an upgrade.
- **Exit:** booked, converted or unsubscribed.
- **Primary metric:** meetings booked or trial activation.
- **Common mistakes:** a sales pitch in the first email, no handoff to a person when the lead replies.

## Regulated industries

If the business is in health, finance, insurance, gambling, alcohol, supplements or anything involving children, use a neutral informational tone: no promises of results, no pressure, no comparisons. Run `compliance-review` with the matching reference pack and ask a qualified professional to review the first version of each template before it is sent.
