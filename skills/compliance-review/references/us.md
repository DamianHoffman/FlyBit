# United States pack

Verification level: the email rules below were checked against the FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide for business. The SMS rules were checked only against law firm commentary, which dates from the time the FCC rule was introduced, and not against the current rule text. Rules and court decisions change, so confirm with a US lawyer.

## Email: CAN-SPAM Act (FTC)

- Applies to all commercial email, including business-to-business email, and not just bulk email.
- The message must include a valid physical postal address.
- The message must clearly explain how to opt out.
- Opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days, and the opt-out mechanism must keep working for at least 30 days after the message is sent.
- Also part of the FTC's list: no false or misleading header information, no deceptive subject lines, and identify the message as an advertisement. You remain responsible for what others send on your behalf.
- The FTC guide states penalties of up to $53,088 per violating email.

## SMS: TCPA (FCC)

- Marketing text messages sent to mobile numbers using automated systems generally need prior express written consent. Consent can be collected electronically, for example through a web form, and the seller carries the burden of proving it.
- A prior business relationship is not a substitute for that consent for marketing texts.
- Do-Not-Call rules apply to texts as well as calls.
- Statutory damages apply per message, and law firm commentary cites $500 per message and up to $1,500 for willful violations. Confirm the current figures.
- Informational (non-marketing) messages are treated differently, but do not label marketing as informational.
- Your SMS provider and the mobile carriers have their own registration and content rules. Check them.
- Some US states add their own rules.

## Industries

For health information, financial services and other regulated areas, talk to a US lawyer about sector rules (for example HIPAA for health data) before sending.

## Questions for a lawyer

1. Does our consent wording meet the current FCC definition of prior express written consent?
2. Do any state laws add requirements for our recipients?
3. Is our opt-out handling, including the 10 business day rule, documented?
