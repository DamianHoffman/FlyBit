# Health communication pack (Poland)

For businesses that provide or promote health services or medicines in Poland, such as clinics, telemedicine and pharmacies. Verified in September 2026 against published legal commentary, and the interpretation of some rules is disputed. This pack gives questions and flags for a lawyer. It is not an opinion.

## Gate 1: features of advertising of medical services

Article 14(1) of the Act on Medical Activity (ustawa o działalności leczniczej) says that the content and form of information about the scope and types of health services must not have the features of advertising. Interpretations differ: some commentators read it as a ban on encouraging people to use health services, others read it more narrowly. Commentators list as features of advertising: encouragement to use a service, promises of effectiveness, comparisons with others, promotions and price emphasis, and time pressure. Violations are described as a misdemeanor. Your role: mark each such element in the text and propose a neutral wording. Whether a given communication fits the rule is for a lawyer.

Default to an informational tone: who is writing, what is available, how to use it, how to opt out. No encouragement, promises, pressure, comparisons or promotions.

## Gate 2: medicines

Article 52(1) of the Pharmaceutical Law (Prawo farmaceutyczne) describes advertising of a medicinal product as informing or encouraging use of it in order to increase prescriptions, supply, sales or consumption. Advertising of prescription-only medicines to the general public is prohibited. In practice: no medicine names or active substances, no encouragement to "renew" a particular treatment, and no promise of a prescription.

## Gate 3: health data and message privacy

Data concerning health is a special category under article 9 of the GDPR. The mere fact that someone uses medical consultations can reveal something, and a third person may see the message on a lock screen or in a shared inbox. So: a sender name that does not suggest a condition, content without the type of service or symptoms, and personalization by first name at most. Ask the data protection officer about the legal basis for segmentation and profiling.

## Content checks

| # | Check | What to look for |
|---|-------|------------------|
| H1 | Forbidden content | No medicine names, substances, diagnoses, symptoms or procedures |
| H2 | Promises | No guarantee of a prescription, sick leave, referral, timing or effectiveness. A doctor decides individually |
| H3 | Pressure | No "last chance", "today only", or scare messages about health consequences |
| H4 | Comparisons and superlatives | No "best", "fastest", or references to competitors |
| H5 | Encouragement | In informational tone, no verbs that push ("use now", "don't wait", "sign up today") |
| H6 | Truthfulness | Everything said about the service can be documented |

## Questions for a lawyer and data protection officer

1. Is the sender a medical entity within the meaning of the Act on Medical Activity, and do reactivation messages fit article 14(1), and to what extent?
2. Does the consent collected at registration cover these messages, separately for email and SMS?
3. Is segmenting by date of last consultation profiling with use of health data, and what is its legal basis?
4. Do tracking pixels and tracked links need extra information or consent?
5. How long and how do we store the list of people who opted out?
